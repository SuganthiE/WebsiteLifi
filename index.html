TECHNOLOGY

What is Li-Fi?

◈

Definition

Li-Fi (Light Fidelity) is a bidirectional, high-speed, and fully networked wireless communication technology that uses visible light, ultraviolet, and infrared spectra for data transmission.

◉

How It Works

Data is encoded by modulating the intensity of LED light at speeds imperceptible to the human eye. A photodiode receiver detects these fluctuations and decodes them back into binary data.

◎

vs Wi-Fi

Li-Fi uses the visible light spectrum (400–800 THz) offering 10,000× more bandwidth than Wi-Fi's radio spectrum, making it ideal for high-density, interference-free environments.

◉ LED Transmitter +
High-brightness LEDs are modulated at frequencies from 1 MHz to several hundred MHz. The intensity variations encode binary data — ON = 1, OFF = 0. OOK (On-Off Keying) or OFDM modulation techniques are used. The LED flickers too fast for human eyes to detect but is captured by the photodiode receiver.
◈ Driver Circuit +
A MOSFET-based gate driver converts the microcontroller's 3.3V/5V PWM signal into high-current LED drive signals. It handles current regulation, protection, and ensures the LED operates within safe power limits while achieving maximum modulation bandwidth.
⬡ Microcontroller +
The ATmega328P or ESP32 handles data encoding, UART communication, PWM generation for LED modulation, error correction, and protocol management. It serializes data into bit streams and manages the transmission timing with hardware timers for precision.
◎ Photodiode Receiver +
A reverse-biased PIN photodiode converts incident photons into electrical current. An operational amplifier (transimpedance amplifier) converts this current to voltage. The signal is then digitized by the microcontroller's ADC for data decoding. Bandwidth: up to 1 GHz with proper design.
